Edge cases — Multi-bin dispense

Companion to Multi-bin dispense — functional user stories. Each entry states the condition, the exact interface reaction, and the copy shown. Entries marked DECISION are not settled in the prototype and need a product call before build.

How to read an entry
When — the trigger condition, observable on screen.
Interface reacts — required behaviour, including state of every affected control.

1. Entering a product

EC-1.1 Product lives in exactly one bin
Single-bin product is opened.
Footer Location reads 1/1 and the stepper is inert. The first bin is also the last, so the dose gate applies immediately and Skip & continue means skipping the product, not the bin.
EC-1.2 Facility cannot cover the configured dose
Total available stock across every bin is below the configured dose.
Detected on entry, not at the last bin. An amber line sits under Configured Dose: "Available 700 mg of 950 mg configured — this order cannot be filled". The technician learns it before walking to the first door.
EC-1.3 Returning to a product already worked on
Technician jumps back to a product they previously left.
Only skipped products can be reopened; the skipped status clears and the product restarts at its first bin. Dispensed products are locked — greyed in the panel, and tapping toasts that they cannot be reopened.

2. Finding and adding serials

EC-2.1 Suggested row belongs to another bin
Serial is listed but sits behind a different door or bin.
Row is greyed, its checkbox is inert and its Add button is muted. Tapping the button toasts "Switch to Door 3 · Bin 12 to dispense this serial" — visible for planning, not actionable from here.
EC-2.2 A single serial overshoots the remaining dose
Adding one more full vial would exceed the configured dose.
The add is refused with "Configured dose reached. Extra serials were not added." To take a partial, the technician adds a serial that fits and edits it down — the balance routes to Waste.
EC-2.3 Bulk selection exceeds the dose
Several serials are checked and Dispense Selected is pressed.
Serials are accepted in order until the next would exceed the target; the rest are dropped with one toast. The selection clears so the technician re-picks deliberately rather than guessing what landed.
EC-2.4 Every serial in the bin is already on the list
Nothing selectable remains in the current bin.
The suggested table empties for this bin and the header checkbox goes inert — grey border, not-allowed cursor, no dash state. The picked serials sit at the top of the dispense list, because current-bin rows sort first.
EC-2.5 Current bin cannot cover the remaining dose
Bin is exhausted but the dose is still short, and other bins hold stock.
The primary action reads "Continue to next bin" and cycles to the next bin holding unpicked stock — earliest expiration first, then partials. Nothing is saved by this click; the Location panel shows the dose already taken from each bin.

3. Scanning

EC-3.1 Serial belongs to another bin
Scanned serial is valid for this product but stored elsewhere.
Not added. Toast names the real location: "That serial is in Door 3 · Bin 12. Switch location first."
EC-3.2 Serial belongs to a different product
Technician scans a vial from another order on the cart.
Distinguished from a genuine miss: "That serial is Rituximab 500 mg/50 mL vial — you are dispensing Avastin 400 mg/16 mL vial". Wrong-drug is the error worth naming loudly.
EC-3.3 Serial is unknown or already dispensed
Barcode is unreadable, mistyped, or the serial is already on the list.
One toast covers both: "Serial not found in available inventory". The field clears so the next scan is not appended to the failed one.
EC-3.4 Same serial scanned twice
Double trigger on the scanner, or a re-scan of a picked vial.
The second scan finds nothing, because dispensed serials leave the available pool. A duplicate row can never be created.
EC-3.5 Empty scan field submitted
Enter pressed with nothing typed.
Nothing happens — no toast, no error state. An empty submit is a slip, not a mistake worth reporting.

4. Editing a dispensed serial

EC-4.1 Value entered exceeds what the vial holds
Vials, dose or volume typed above the serial's inventory.
Values clamp to the serial's inventory. A 0.5-vial serial snaps back to 0.5 / 50 mg / 5 mL, and all three fields update together so they can never disagree.
EC-4.2 Negative or cleared value
Field emptied mid-typing, or a minus figure entered.
Negatives clamp to zero. An empty field is left alone while typing rather than force-filled, so the technician can clear and retype without fighting the input.
EC-4.3 Edit would push the total past the configured dose
A row is raised so the running total would exceed the target.
Save refuses with "That would exceed the configured dose by 50 mg" and the row stays in edit mode so the value can be corrected. The clamp is per dose, not just per serial.
EC-4.4 Footer pressed mid-edit
A row is open in edit mode.
Both footer actions are disabled — the bin cannot be committed on a half-typed value. Save or Cancel restores them.
EC-4.5 Editing a serial from another bin
Row on the dispense list was picked in an earlier bin.
Permitted — nothing is saved yet, so correcting any row's numbers is safe from any bin. Remove stays bin-restricted, since it implies physically returning the vial: it is only enabled for serials in the current door and bin.
EC-4.6 Serial edited down to zero
Dispense amount set to 0 mg on a picked vial.
Allowed; the row stays on the list contributing 0 mg with the full vial as waste. Removing the serial outright is the cleaner action and remains available for current-bin rows.

5. Moving between bins

EC-5.1 Switching bins with picked or edited serials
A bin is picked from the Location panel while another bin holds unsaved work.
Switches immediately — no prompt. Nothing was saved in the previous bin to begin with, so there is nothing to discard; everything picked or edited stays in local state and travels with the product to the new bin.
EC-5.2 Jumping to a different product mid-dispense
Product panel is used to open another product while the current one is unfinished.
Also switches immediately, for the same reason — no save-or-discard checkpoint exists because nothing has been written yet. The unfinished product simply waits, reopenable later like a skipped one.
EC-5.3 Destination bin is behind a different door
The next bin, or the next product's first bin, sits behind another door.
Toast "Door 3 is unlocked now". If the previous door is still open, a "Door is Open" prompt asks for it to be closed first and the pending bin is applied on OK. Same-door bin changes are silent; for demo purposes the prompt fires once per session.
EC-5.4 Returning to a bin already picked from
Technician steps back to an earlier bin.
Permitted. Those serials become actionable again — remove and edit re-enable, the "Dispensed" marker disappears, and they sort back to the top of the list.
EC-5.5 Later bins hold nothing left to pick
Advance is triggered but the remaining bins are empty.
The advance skips them; if none remain the product finalises instead. The technician is never sent to an empty bin.

6. Committing and skipping

EC-6.0 Which bin "Continue to next bin" picks
More than one bin still holds unpicked stock for this product.
Ranked by earliest expiration across all remaining bins, then by whether the bin holds a partial vial — same-door bins get no priority over others, so the promise stays "soonest to expire" regardless of which door that means opening next. A tweak can fall back to plain bin-list order.
EC-6.1 Last bin reached with the dose still short
No bin holds unpicked stock and the dispensed total is below the configured dose.
"Continue to next bin" cycles back through the bin list rather than disabling. If every bin has been visited and the shortfall is real, it toasts "600 of 950 mg picked. The full configured dose must be dispensed before continuing." There is no partial-dispense path, but the button itself is never a dead end.
EC-6.2 Skip attempted after any interaction
A serial has been added, edited, or a bulk action applied, in any bin for this product.
Skip is disabled immediately — not just once something is added, but from the first interaction with any serial. Only removing every added serial and undoing every edit, back to the product's initial state, re-enables it.
EC-6.3 Skip is always a whole-product decision
Skip & continue is pressed at any point in the flow.
There is no per-bin meaning left to distinguish — since nothing saves until the full dose, Skip always means abandoning the product, and only ever from a pristine one (see EC-6.2).
EC-6.4 Skipping the product
Skip pressed on a product with nothing added or edited.
"Are you sure you want to skip this product?" naming the product, with NO, STAY and YES, CONTINUE. Confirming marks it skipped and goes straight to the next product — no interim modal. Copy stays "Skip & continue" throughout; the bin-versus-product distinction is behavioural, never verbal.
EC-6.5 Last remaining product is finished or skipped
Nothing is left to advance to.
The run closes with "Dispense workflow complete", reporting dispensed and skipped counts. Completing an earlier product only toasts "<Product> is successfully dispensed" — the end of the run is the one place a modal is justified.
EC-6.6 Dose met before the last bin, on the last product
The checked selection meets the configured dose, on the last remaining product.
The primary action flips live from "CONTINUE TO NEXT BIN" straight to "DISPENSE & VIEW SUMMARY", skipping the mid-state "DISPENSE & CONTINUE" entirely, since there is no next product to continue to. The label always describes the outcome of the click, never the position in the list.

7. Session and live data

EC-7.1 Leaving the order mid-run
Top bar or left rail navigation is used while a bin holds uncommitted work.
Out of scope for the demo — the top bar and left rail are inert throughout, with no visual distinction from an active state. The only way out of a product is the footer.
EC-7.2 Another technician takes stock mid-run DECISION
A suggested serial is removed from its bin by someone else before it is picked.
The prototype assumes a static pool. Proposed: the row falls out of the table with a toast rather than failing at commit. Needs a call on polling frequency and on what happens if the serial was already added.
